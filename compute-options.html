The option in every block

A miner who finds a block does not simply receive a reward. They choose which reward to receive — QUAI, the monetary token, or Qi, the energy token. That choice is an option, and it has a name.

01

The election

At the moment a block is found, the miner elects to mint QUAI or Qi. The payoff of that choice is the greater of the two values — max(QUAI, Qi). That is not a metaphor for an option. It is the payoff function of an exchange option, exactly.

02

The forced exercise

The election is fixed at block time. The miner cannot wait, cannot sell the right, cannot let it run. They are compelled to exercise immediately, at intrinsic value, and everything the option was worth beyond that is destroyed.

03

The waste

Time value is the entire reason options are worth more than their immediate payoff. Forced exercise at block time burns all of it — every block, at every miner, permanently. That destroyed value is the raw material for everything on this page.

One contract, two names

People say QUAI is a call on Qi, and that Qi is a put on QUAI. Both are true — but they are not two facts. They are the same contract described from two sides, and understanding why is the whole design.

Priced in Qi — energy as the unit

It looks like a call

Hold Qi, and you hold the right to acquire QUAI. Energy is the unit you count in, so the payoff rises as QUAI gets more expensive in energy terms. Every desk would book this as a call.

Priced in QUAI — money as the unit

It looks like a put

Flip the unit. Count in QUAI instead, and the identical contract now pays off as Qi gets cheaper. Nothing about the contract changed — only the denominator. Every desk would book this as a put.

max(A, B)  =  B + (A − B)+  =  A + (B − A)+

This is the identity behind the block election. It is standard in currency markets — a dollar call is a yen put, the same ticket sold twice. The practical consequence matters: you cannot build a straddle by holding both tokens. Holding QUAI and Qi is holding two underlyings. Two contracts are needed, and someone has to write them.

What an LMT actually is

A zero-coupon bond, not an option

Because the QUAI-or-Qi election is fixed at block time, a deferred reward is a claim on a known quantity of a known token at a known date. There is no choice left inside it. An LMT is a bond — and that is a feature, because two bonds in two units give you something no single token can.

Deferred Qi claims form one curve. Deferred QUAI claims form another. Qi is minted against difficulty-adjusted work, so the Qi curve is a real rate — the price of energy through time. The QUAI curve is nominal. The gap between them is a breakeven: the market's live estimate of how fast monetary premium is growing against physical work.

A falling breakeven is the earliest available warning that premium is decaying relative to energy. It is a measurement rather than an assertion, and it exists as soon as both LMT curves trade.

Open design question

The ladder above is inverted at the front. Rolling the three-month four times compounds to 46.4% against 25% for locking twelve months — so under a static schedule the long tenor never prints. Either the accretion rates become controller-set, or the long tenors have to carry something the short ones do not. That is a live question, not a settled one.

Convertible LMTs

Here is where the option comes back. A convertible LMT is a deferred Qi claim that the holder may convert into QUAI at a fixed ratio at maturity — or the mirror image, deferred QUAI convertible into Qi.

01

The miner writes it

The miner accepts a lower accretion rate in exchange for granting the conversion right. They are selling time value they were going to destroy anyway at block time. Nothing is manufactured — a right that already existed is simply made assignable.

02

Two strips fall out

A convertible decomposes cleanly into a bond floor plus an exchange option. Strip the floor away and you are left with the option alone: energy-denominated downside protection on one side, the pure conversion right on the other.

03

It prints a price

Every convertible that trades reveals what the market pays for the QUAI-versus-Qi conversion right. That is an observed implied volatility on the pair — a number the protocol currently has no closed form for and no way to measure.

Building the straddle

One convertible gives you half the shape. Buy the Qi-side convertible and the QUAI-side convertible together, strip both floors, and size them to the same notional — and the straddle finally exists, because two separate counterparties wrote the two halves.

Why this is the point

A straddle is not a speculative flourish here. It is the only instrument that prices something the network otherwise has to assert on faith.

Convexity cannot be created — only relocated

Every straddle needs a writer, and the writer must be funded

A price floor a protocol simply promises is, in options terms, a written put with no collateral behind it. That is precisely the structure that spirals: the guarantee is backed by the same system whose failure would trigger it. Naming it that way makes the failure mode legible instead of mysterious.

A convertible LMT is different in kind. The conversion right is written by a specific miner, against a specific deferred reward that already exists, in exchange for a specific reduction in yield. It is a funded, contractual position. Nobody is short an option they have not been paid for.

And once the two convertibles trade, the spread between them is a live, observable price of volatility on QUAI against Qi. Floor belief stops being a matter of argument and becomes a market quote that anyone can read.
